# Study in the USA: Complete Roadmap for Indian Students

The United States is one of the world's major international education destinations. Its universities offer undergraduate degrees, postgraduate programmes, research opportunities, professional courses and interdisciplinary learning.

However, studying in the USA requires careful planning. Tuition, living expenses, university admission requirements, scholarships and immigration rules vary significantly. This guide explains the process for Indian students, from choosing a course to preparing for life after graduation.

*Last reviewed: November 2026. Always verify current admission, fee and immigration requirements with the relevant university and official government authorities.*

---

## 1. Why Study in the USA?

The USA offers several advantages for students who want an internationally recognised education.

- **Wide programme selection:** Choose from engineering, computer science, business, medicine-related fields, arts, sciences, law and many specialised disciplines.
- **Research opportunities:** Many universities offer research laboratories, faculty-led projects and graduate assistantships.
- **Flexible academic pathways:** Some institutions allow students to explore subjects before finalising a major.
- **Industry exposure:** Universities may offer internships, career fairs, employer networking and practical learning.
- **Campus diversity:** Students interact with people from different countries, cultures and academic backgrounds.
- **Specialised programmes:** Options include artificial intelligence, robotics, biotechnology, finance, public policy and many emerging fields.

The best university is not necessarily the highest-ranked one. It is the institution that matches your academic goals, budget and career plans.

## 2. Understand the Different Study Options

Indian students generally consider three major academic routes.

### A. Undergraduate study

A Bachelor's degree usually takes four years in the USA.

Popular programmes include:

- Computer Science
- Mechanical Engineering
- Electrical Engineering
- Civil Engineering
- Aerospace Engineering
- Business Administration
- Economics
- Psychology
- Biology
- Mathematics
- Data Science
- Architecture
- Design

Students generally apply after completing Class 12.

### B. Postgraduate study

A Master's degree commonly takes one to two years, depending on the programme.

Popular options include:

- MS in Computer Science
- MS in Data Science
- MS in Artificial Intelligence
- MS in Mechanical Engineering
- MS in Electrical Engineering
- MS in Civil Engineering
- MS in Business Analytics
- MBA
- MS in Finance
- MS in Biotechnology
- MS in Cybersecurity
- MS in Public Health

Admission requirements depend on the university, programme and applicant's academic background.

### C. Doctoral study

A PhD is intended for students interested in advanced research, academic careers or specialised research and development roles.

Doctoral programmes may provide funding through research assistantships, teaching assistantships, fellowships or scholarships. Funding is programme-specific and should never be assumed.

## 3. Eligibility for Indian Students

There is no single admission standard for every American university.

Universities evaluate applicants using a combination of academic performance, subject preparation, test scores where required, extracurricular activities, recommendations and other application materials.

### Undergraduate admission

Common requirements include:

- Class 10 and Class 12 academic records
- School transcripts and certificates
- English-language proficiency where required
- SAT or ACT scores if required or considered by the university
- Personal essays or statements
- Letters of recommendation
- Extracurricular activities and achievements
- Financial documentation

Some universities are test-optional, while others require standardised tests. Always check the current policy for your intended admission year.

### Postgraduate admission

Common requirements include:

- Recognised Bachelor's degree
- Academic transcripts
- Relevant subject prerequisites
- English-language proficiency
- GRE or GMAT where required
- Statement of Purpose
- Letters of Recommendation
- Resume or CV
- Portfolio, research proposal or writing sample for selected programmes
- Relevant work or research experience where required

For engineering programmes, universities may examine mathematics, programming and core technical subjects.

For business programmes, they may evaluate quantitative ability, professional experience, leadership and communication skills.

**Important:** Meeting the minimum eligibility requirements does not guarantee admission.

## 4. Entrance Exams and English-Language Tests

The required tests depend on the institution and programme.

### SAT

The SAT may be required or considered for undergraduate admission.

It assesses skills in areas including:

- Reading and writing
- Mathematics

Check whether each university requires, recommends or does not consider SAT scores.

### ACT

The ACT is another undergraduate admissions test accepted by many institutions. Requirements and testing policies vary.

### GRE

The Graduate Record Examination may be required for selected postgraduate programmes, particularly in some technical, scientific and academic disciplines.

Many universities have waived or made GRE scores optional for certain courses.

### GMAT

The GMAT is commonly associated with business and management programmes, including some MBA courses.

Not every business school requires it.

### English-language tests

Commonly accepted tests include:

- IELTS Academic
- TOEFL iBT
- PTE Academic
- Duolingo English Test, where accepted

Universities establish their own minimum scores and accepted test formats. Some offer exemptions based on previous education in English.

Do not book a test before checking the requirements of your shortlisted universities.

## 5. How to Choose the Right University

University selection is one of the most important decisions in the entire process.

Do not select a university based only on its global ranking.

Evaluate the following factors:

1. Course curriculum and specialisation options
2. Accreditation and institutional recognition
3. Faculty expertise and research facilities
4. Internship and industry connections
5. Graduate employment outcomes
6. Total tuition and living expenses
7. Scholarships and assistantships
8. Location and local cost of living
9. Campus facilities and student support
10. International student services

### Types of universities

**Public universities:** Often supported by state governments. Costs and admission policies vary.

**Private universities:** Independently funded institutions. Some have substantial institutional scholarship programmes, while others are expensive.

**Community colleges:** Typically offer two-year associate degrees and may provide transfer pathways to four-year universities. Confirm that a transfer agreement and the intended bachelor's pathway are suitable before enrolling.

## 6. Estimated Cost of Studying in the USA

The USA can be expensive, particularly at private universities and in major metropolitan areas.

Your budget should include:

- Tuition and academic fees
- Accommodation
- Food and groceries
- Health insurance
- Transport
- Books and learning materials
- Personal expenses
- Travel
- Visa and administrative costs

### Illustrative annual budget

The following figures are broad planning estimates in US dollars, not official fee quotations.

| Expense | Illustrative annual range |
|---|---:|
| Tuition | $15,000–$60,000+ |
| Accommodation | $7,000–$18,000 |
| Food | $3,000–$6,000 |
| Transport | $500–$2,500 |
| Health insurance | $1,000–$3,000 |
| Books and personal expenses | $1,500–$4,000 |

Actual costs may be substantially higher or lower depending on the university, programme, location and lifestyle.

### How to reduce expenses

- Apply to universities offering merit scholarships.
- Compare public and private university costs.
- Consider universities outside expensive metropolitan areas.
- Investigate graduate assistantships.
- Check whether accommodation and meals are included in the university's cost estimate.
- Compare the total cost of the entire degree, not just the first semester.

Never assume that part-time work will cover your tuition or living expenses.

## 7. Scholarships and Financial Aid

Scholarships can make American education more affordable.

### Common funding options

**Merit-based scholarships:** Awarded based on academic performance, achievements, leadership or other institutional criteria.

**Need-based financial aid:** Offered by selected institutions according to demonstrated financial circumstances and eligibility rules.

**Graduate assistantships:** Students may receive funding in exchange for teaching, research or other university responsibilities.

**Research funding:** Certain research-oriented Master's and PhD programmes provide financial support.

**External scholarships:** Private foundations, organisations and government programmes may support eligible students.

### Scholarship application strategy

1. Prepare a list of universities offering funding.
2. Check international student eligibility.
3. Review scholarship deadlines separately from admission deadlines.
4. Prepare academic records and recommendation letters.
5. Write a strong personal statement.
6. Contact departments about research or assistantship opportunities.
7. Confirm whether the scholarship covers tuition, living expenses or only part of the cost.

A scholarship advertised by a university may not be available to every international applicant.

## 8. Application Process: Step by Step

American university applications may require significant preparation.

### Step 1: Identify your course

Decide what you want to study and why.

Compare programme content, specialisations and career outcomes.

### Step 2: Shortlist universities

Prepare a balanced shortlist:

- Ambitious options
- Universities that closely match your profile
- More accessible options

Do not rely entirely on one university.

### Step 3: Check admission requirements

For each university, record:

- Academic eligibility
- Required subjects
- Minimum grades or GPA
- Standardised tests
- English-language requirements
- Application deadlines
- Tuition
- Scholarship deadlines

### Step 4: Prepare application documents

Common documents include:

- Passport
- Class 10 and Class 12 certificates
- Undergraduate transcripts, if applicable
- Degree certificate, if applicable
- Test scores
- Statement of Purpose or personal essay
- Letters of Recommendation
- Resume or CV
- Portfolio, where required
- Financial documents, where required

### Step 5: Submit applications

Apply through the university's official portal or an application platform accepted by the institution.

Check that every document has been uploaded correctly.

### Step 6: Track admission decisions

Universities may offer:

- Admission
- Conditional admission
- Waitlist placement
- Deferral
- Rejection

Read every offer carefully.

### Step 7: Compare offers

Compare the total cost, financial aid, course structure, university support and career opportunities.

Do not accept an offer simply because the university has a famous name.

## 9. Understanding the F-1 Student Visa

Most Indian students pursuing full-time academic degrees at American universities apply for an **F-1 student visa**.

Vocational or other recognised non-academic programmes may fall under the M-1 category. Exchange programmes may use the J-1 category.

### Basic F-1 process

1. Obtain admission from a SEVP-approved institution.
2. Receive your Form I-20.
3. Pay the required SEVIS I-901 fee.
4. Complete the DS-160 online visa application.
5. Pay the applicable visa application fee.
6. Schedule the required visa appointment.
7. Attend the interview with the required documents.
8. Follow the instructions for passport return and travel.

### Important documents

- Valid passport
- Form I-20
- DS-160 confirmation
- Visa fee payment receipt, where applicable
- SEVIS fee payment confirmation
- Admission documentation
- Academic transcripts and certificates
- Standardised test scores, where applicable
- Evidence of funds for tuition and living expenses
- Other documents requested by the consular officer

The visa officer may ask about your academic preparation, course selection, funding and study plans.

Answer truthfully and clearly. Never use fabricated financial documents or false statements.

### Visa fee

The US Department of State currently lists a $185 application processing fee for F-category student visas. Additional applicable charges and procedures should be checked before applying.

Official visa information: https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/study/student-visa.html

After accepting admission and completing the institution's requirements:

- [ ] Confirm that the university is SEVP-certified.

- [ ] Verify the SEVIS identification number.

- [ ] Pay the I-901 SEVIS fee through the official payment system.
- [ ] Save the SEVIS payment confirmation.
- [ ] Contact the university if any information on the I-20 is incorrect.

The current I-901 SEVIS fee for F-1 students is **US$350**. Verify the amount and payment instructions before paying.

### 5. F-1 Student Visa Application

- [ ] Check the latest instructions from the US Embassy or Consulate in India.
- [ ] Complete Form DS-160 accurately.
- [ ] Save and print the DS-160 confirmation page.
- [ ] Arrange a compliant visa photograph.
- [ ] Pay the applicable visa application fee.
- [ ] Schedule the visa appointment according to current procedures.
- [ ] Prepare your passport and Form I-20.
- [ ] Carry the SEVIS I-901 fee payment confirmation.
- [ ] Prepare academic certificates and transcripts.
- [ ] Prepare standardised test results, where relevant.
- [ ] Prepare evidence of funds for tuition, living expenses and travel.
- [ ] Keep scholarship or assistantship award letters, if applicable.
- [ ] Review your university, programme and funding information before the interview.

### 6. F-1 Visa Interview Preparation

Be prepared to explain:

- [ ] Why you selected the United States.
- [ ] Why you chose the particular university.
- [ ] How the programme relates to your academic background.
- [ ] What you intend to study.
- [ ] How you will finance your education.
- [ ] What your academic and career plans are.
- [ ] How you understand the conditions of F-1 student status.

Answer honestly and consistently with your application documents. Do not memorise fabricated answers or submit false financial evidence.

### 7. Before Departure

- [ ] Confirm that your passport and visa details are correct.
- [ ] Check your Form I-20 programme start date.
- [ ] Confirm university accommodation arrangements.
- [ ] Arrange health insurance according to university requirements.
- [ ] Book travel after considering visa approval and entry dates.
- [ ] Carry your passport, visa and Form I-20.
- [ ] Carry important academic and financial documents.
- [ ] Keep university and emergency contact details accessible.
- [ ] Review customs and entry requirements.
- [ ] Understand your university's arrival and reporting procedures.

### Important F-1 Travel Rules

- New F-1 student visas may generally be issued up to 365 days before the programme start date.
- New students generally cannot enter the United States more than 30 days before the programme start date shown on Form I-20.
- A visa does not guarantee admission at the US port of entry.
- Students must maintain their F-1 status and follow employment authorisation requirements.

## 10. Working While Studying

International students in F-1 status have strict employment restrictions.

### On-campus employment

Eligible F-1 students can generally work on campus subject to immigration rules.

During an academic term, on-campus employment is generally limited to 20 hours per week. Full-time work may be permitted during authorised school breaks, subject to the applicable rules.

### Curricular Practical Training (CPT)

CPT allows eligible students to undertake practical training, internships or cooperative education that forms an integral part of their established curriculum.

Important points:

- The training must relate directly to the student's major.
- The student must meet eligibility requirements.
- The Designated School Official must authorise CPT in SEVIS.
- The authorisation must be issued before employment begins.
- CPT is tied to the specific employer and authorised dates.

One year or more of full-time CPT can eliminate eligibility for post-completion OPT.

### Optional Practical Training (OPT)

OPT allows eligible F-1 students to undertake temporary employment directly related to their field of study.

Post-completion OPT is generally available for up to 12 months, subject to eligibility and approval.

### STEM OPT extension

Eligible graduates with qualifying STEM degrees may apply for a 24-month extension of post-completion OPT, subject to requirements including employer participation and other conditions.

This can provide a potential total of up to 36 months of post-completion OPT for qualifying students.

**Important:** CPT and OPT are not automatic employment rights. Students must follow the applicable authorisation process and must not begin work before receiving the required authorisation.

The USA offers opportunities across many industries, but employment is not guaranteed.

Possible career sectors include:

- Cybersecurity

### Engineering

- Mechanical design
- Robotics
- Automotive engineering

### Business and finance

- Business analytics
- Financial analysis
- Consulting
- Marketing
- Operations
- Supply chain management

### Healthcare and life sciences

- Biotechnology
- Biomedical research
- Public health
- Pharmaceutical research
- Healthcare analytics

Professional licensing requirements apply to several occupations, particularly in healthcare, law and selected engineering roles.

### What happens after OPT?

Students who want to remain in the USA after their authorised practical training period need to qualify for another immigration status or otherwise comply with the applicable departure requirements.

Some graduates may pursue employer-sponsored work visas, further education or other eligible immigration pathways.

Do not choose a university based on an assumption that permanent residence or a particular work visa will automatically follow graduation.

## 12. Building a Strong Profile Before Applying

Admission committees evaluate more than academic marks.

Start building your profile well before the application season.

### For engineering students

- Develop programming and technical skills.
- Complete practical projects.
- Participate in technical competitions.
- Build a portfolio or GitHub profile.
- Seek internships and research exposure.
- Strengthen mathematics and core engineering subjects.

### For business students

- Develop communication and analytical skills.
- Participate in leadership activities.
- Build business or market research projects.
- Gain internship experience.
- Learn relevant analytical tools.

### For science students

- Participate in research projects.
- Develop laboratory and analytical skills.
- Build relationships with faculty members.
- Explore research internships and publications where appropriate.

### For all applicants

- Maintain consistent academic performance.
- Develop strong writing skills.
- Participate in meaningful extracurricular activities.
- Build a clear academic and career direction.
- Request recommendations from people who know your work.

Quality matters more than collecting certificates without meaningful involvement.

## 14. Common Mistakes Indian Students Should Avoid

1. Selecting universities only by ranking.
2. Ignoring the total cost of attendance.
3. Assuming every university offers scholarships.
4. Applying without checking programme prerequisites.
5. Treating GRE, GMAT, SAT or ACT as universally mandatory.
6. Writing generic application essays.
7. Submitting weak or unsuitable recommendation letters.
8. Depending on part-time work to finance the entire degree.
9. Assuming internships and off-campus jobs are automatically permitted.
10. Trusting agents who promise guaranteed admission or visas.
11. Ignoring university accreditation and recognition.
12. Making travel arrangements before visa approval.
