**Build a professional reputation, create meaningful connections and communicate with confidence.**

Career development is not based only on qualifications and technical knowledge. How you communicate, interact with people, present yourself and build professional relationships can also influence your opportunities.

Professional etiquette helps you behave appropriately in different professional situations. Networking helps you build meaningful relationships. Personal branding helps others understand your strengths, interests and professional value.

Together, these skills can support your transition from education into employment and help you develop throughout your career.

---

## 1. What Is Professional Etiquette?

Professional etiquette refers to the accepted standards of behaviour, communication and conduct in educational and workplace environments.

It includes:
- Respecting other people's time.
- Communicating clearly and politely.
- Maintaining appropriate workplace behaviour.
- Being punctual and dependable.
- Respecting professional boundaries.
- Dressing appropriately for the situation.
- Handling disagreements respectfully.
- Maintaining confidentiality.

Professional etiquette is not about following unnecessary formalities. It is about demonstrating consideration, reliability and respect.

## 2. Why Professional Etiquette Matters

Professional behaviour can influence how people experience working with you.

**For students:**
- Making a positive impression during internships.
- Communicating with professors and mentors.
- Participating in placement activities.
- Interacting with visiting recruiters.
- Working effectively in project teams.

**For graduates:**
- Preparing for interviews.
- Communicating with recruiters.
- Building professional relationships.
- Adjusting to workplace expectations.
- Representing themselves professionally.

**For young professionals:**
- Building credibility.
- Working with clients and colleagues.
- Participating in meetings.
- Managing workplace disagreements.
- Developing leadership opportunities.

Your professional reputation develops through repeated interactions, not one impressive introduction.

## 3. Workplace Communication Etiquette

Clear communication is one of the foundations of professional behaviour.

### 3.1 Email Etiquette

A professional email should be clear, concise and respectful.

Important practices:
- Use a meaningful subject line.
- Address the recipient appropriately.
- State the purpose early.
- Organise information into readable paragraphs.
- Avoid unnecessary informal language.
- Check spelling and grammar.
- Attach the correct documents.
- Include an appropriate closing.
- Avoid sending confidential information to unintended recipients.

**Example email structure:**

Subject: Request for Internship Application Update

Dear [Recipient's Name],

I hope you are doing well.

I am writing to enquire about the status of my internship application submitted on [date].

I would appreciate any update you could share regarding the next steps.

Thank you for your time.

Regards,  
[Your Name]

### 3.2 Meeting Etiquette

Professional meetings require preparation and consideration.

Before a meeting:
- Review the agenda.
- Understand the purpose.
- Prepare relevant questions.
- Join on time.

During a meeting:
- Listen carefully.
- Avoid interrupting.
- Contribute when relevant.
- Keep comments focused.
- Respect different opinions.

After a meeting:
- Review the decisions.
- Complete assigned tasks.
- Share relevant updates.
- Follow agreed deadlines.

### 3.3 Messaging Etiquette

Workplace messaging platforms are useful for quick communication, but they should be used responsibly.

- Keep messages relevant.
- Avoid unnecessary repeated notifications.
- Respect working hours and communication boundaries.
- Do not assume an immediate response is always possible.
- Avoid sharing sensitive information in inappropriate channels.
- Use a professional tone.

### 3.4 Video Meeting Etiquette

For virtual meetings:
- Test your microphone and internet connection.
- Use an appropriate background.
- Keep your camera settings suitable for the meeting.
- Mute yourself when not speaking.
- Avoid multitasking unnecessarily.
- Be attentive and participate appropriately.

## 4. Workplace Behaviour and Professional Conduct

Professionalism is demonstrated through everyday actions.

### Punctuality

Arrive on time for meetings, interviews and agreed commitments.

If you expect a delay, communicate it as early as possible.

### Reliability

Complete assigned work and communicate honestly when deadlines may be affected.

### Respect

Treat colleagues, clients, support staff and managers with courtesy.

### Confidentiality

Do not share private workplace information, customer records or internal documents without authorisation.

### Constructive Feedback

Give feedback about work and behaviour rather than attacking individuals.

### Professional Boundaries

Maintain appropriate relationships and respect personal and organisational boundaries.

## 5. Professional Etiquette for Job Interviews

Interviews are opportunities to demonstrate both competence and professional conduct.

Before the interview:
- Research the organisation.
- Review the job description.
- Prepare examples of relevant experience.
- Check the interview format.
- Prepare thoughtful questions.
- Confirm the time and location or meeting link.

During the interview:
- Join or arrive on time.
- Introduce yourself clearly.
- Listen to the complete question.
- Answer honestly.
- Use relevant examples.
- Avoid speaking negatively about previous organisations or colleagues.
- Ask appropriate questions.

After the interview:
- Thank the interviewers.
- Follow any requested next steps.
- Send a concise follow-up when appropriate.

Professional etiquette cannot compensate for missing qualifications, but it helps ensure that your communication does not create avoidable barriers.

## 6. What Is Networking?

Networking is the process of building and maintaining professional relationships through genuine communication, shared interests and mutual support.

It is not simply collecting contacts or asking people for jobs.

Effective networking involves:
- Learning from others.
- Sharing useful information.
- Discovering opportunities.
- Building professional relationships.
- Supporting other people's development.
- Staying connected over time.

Networking can happen through college activities, professional events, internships, online communities and workplace interactions.

## 7. Why Networking Matters

Professional relationships can help you:
- Discover career paths.
- Learn about industry expectations.
- Find mentors.
- Identify internship opportunities.
- Understand emerging skills.
- Receive career guidance.
- Develop collaborations.
- Gain exposure to different professional experiences.

Networking does not guarantee employment. It helps you build relationships that may create learning and opportunity over time.

## 8. How Students Can Start Networking

You do not need to attend expensive conferences to begin.

Start with accessible opportunities.

**College activities:**
- Join student clubs.
- Participate in technical events.
- Attend seminars and workshops.
- Volunteer for competitions.
- Work on collaborative projects.

**Professional communities:**
- Join relevant online groups.
- Attend industry webinars.
- Participate in hackathons.
- Follow professional associations.
- Engage with useful discussions.

**Mentorship:**
- Approach teachers and alumni respectfully.
- Ask specific questions.
- Be prepared before requesting guidance.
- Respect the mentor's time.
- Apply useful advice and share progress.

## 9. How to Introduce Yourself Professionally

A short professional introduction is often called an elevator pitch.

It should communicate:
1. Who you are.
2. What you study or do.
3. Your relevant interests or strengths.
4. What you are currently working towards.

**Example for a student:**

"Hello, I am a mechanical engineering student interested in product design and manufacturing technology. I am currently developing my technical foundation through academic projects and learning CAD tools. I am interested in understanding how engineering teams approach real-world design challenges."

**Example for a recent graduate:**

"Hello, I recently graduated in commerce and am interested in financial analysis and business operations. I have been developing my spreadsheet and analytical skills through practical projects. I am looking for opportunities to apply these skills in a professional environment."

Keep your introduction natural and accurate. Avoid exaggerating your experience.

## 10. Building a Professional Network Online

Digital platforms allow students and professionals to maintain professional connections.

### LinkedIn

LinkedIn can help you:
- Present your professional background.
- Connect with industry professionals.
- Follow organisations.
- Share project updates.
- Discover job and internship opportunities.
- Participate in professional discussions.

### GitHub

GitHub is particularly useful for technology-related projects.

You can:
- Publish projects.
- Document your work.
- Contribute to open-source repositories.
- Collaborate with developers.
- Demonstrate your technical interests.

### Professional Communities

Depending on your field, you can also participate in:
- Engineering communities.
- Research groups.
- Design communities.
- Business forums.
- Professional associations.
- Industry-specific discussion groups.

Focus on meaningful participation rather than the number of connections.

## 11. What Is Personal Branding?

Personal branding is the way your skills, experience, interests, values and professional contributions are understood by others.

It answers an important question:

**What do people associate with your professional identity?**

For example, a student might develop a professional identity around mechanical design, practical engineering projects and manufacturing technology.

A marketing graduate might focus on digital marketing, content strategy and analytical thinking.

Personal branding is not about pretending to be an expert. It is about communicating your genuine strengths and showing evidence of your development.

## 12. Building Your Personal Brand

### 12.1 Identify Your Professional Interests

Start by identifying:
- Subjects you enjoy.
- Skills you are developing.
- Industries that interest you.
- Problems you want to solve.
- Experiences that distinguish your learning journey.

Your professional interests can evolve as you gain experience.

### 12.2 Build Evidence of Your Skills

Do not rely only on claims.

Demonstrate your abilities through:
- Projects.
- Internships.
- Research.
- Presentations.
- Certifications.
- Open-source contributions.
- Practical work samples.
- Documented achievements.

### 12.3 Maintain a Professional Online Presence

Review your public profiles.

Check whether:
- Your profile information is accurate.
- Your photograph is appropriate for the platform.
- Your headline communicates your interests.
- Your experience is presented honestly.
- Your project links work.
- Your public content reflects professional judgement.

You do not need to publish constantly. Consistency and quality matter more than volume.

### 12.4 Share Useful Knowledge

You can develop your professional presence by sharing:
- Lessons from projects.
- Technical explanations.
- Industry observations.
- Book or course recommendations.
- Reflections on professional learning.
- Useful resources.

Avoid presenting other people's work as your own. Attribute sources and respect copyright.

## 13. Networking Messages: Practical Examples

### Example 1: Connecting with an Industry Professional

Hello [Name],

I came across your work in [industry or field] and found your professional journey interesting. I am currently exploring opportunities in this area and would be glad to connect and learn from your experience.

Thank you.

### Example 2: Requesting Career Guidance

Hello [Name],

I am a student interested in [career field]. I have been exploring the skills and qualifications required to enter this industry.

If you have time, I would appreciate a brief conversation or any resources you recommend for someone beginning this journey.

Thank you for considering my request.

### Example 3: Following Up After an Event

Hello [Name],

It was a pleasure meeting you at [event name]. I found our discussion about [topic] useful.

I would be glad to stay connected and follow your work.

Regards,  
[Your Name]

Keep messages personalised, respectful and concise. Do not repeatedly contact someone who has not responded.

## 14. Common Networking and Personal Branding Mistakes

**1. Connecting only when you need something**

Professional relationships should not be treated as one-time transactions.

**2. Sending generic messages**

A personalised message demonstrates genuine interest.

**3. Exaggerating achievements**

Misrepresenting experience can damage credibility.

**4. Focusing only on follower counts**

A large audience does not automatically create a strong professional reputation.

**5. Sharing everything publicly**

Protect personal information and respect professional confidentiality.

**6. Ignoring existing relationships**

Maintaining connections is as important as making new ones.

**7. Asking for jobs immediately**

Start by establishing context and building a genuine professional conversation.

**8. Treating personal branding as self-promotion alone**

Your reputation is shaped by the quality of your work and how you treat others.

| Period | Activity | Expected outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–5 | Review professional communication and workplace etiquette | Better professional conduct |
| Days 6–10 | Prepare and practise a short professional introduction | Clear self-presentation |
| Days 11–15 | Improve your LinkedIn or relevant professional profile | Stronger online presence |
| Days 16–20 | Connect with relevant professionals and alumni | Initial professional network |
| Days 21–25 | Publish or document one useful project or learning experience | Evidence of skills |
| Days 26–30 | Follow up with contacts and review your professional goals | Sustainable networking habits |

The objective is to develop genuine professional relationships, not to achieve an arbitrary number of connections.

## 16. Professional Etiquette and Personal Branding Self-Assessment

Rate yourself honestly.

- I communicate clearly and respectfully.
- I respect other people's time.
- I behave professionally during meetings.
- I can introduce myself confidently.
- I listen carefully during conversations.
- I respect professional boundaries.
- I maintain an accurate professional profile.
- I can explain my skills and interests.
- I have documented examples of my work.
- I build relationships without expecting immediate benefits.
- I follow up appropriately after professional interactions.
- I respect confidentiality and intellectual property.

Use this scale:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Needs significant improvement |
| 2 | Developing |
| 3 | Satisfactory |
| 4 | Strong |
| 5 | Consistently demonstrated |

This is a personal reflection exercise, not a validated professional assessment.

## Conclusion

Professional etiquette, networking and personal branding help you build credibility and communicate your professional value.

Start by developing respectful communication habits, participating in relevant communities and documenting your skills through real work.

You do not need to know everyone in your industry. Focus on building meaningful relationships, maintaining a trustworthy reputation and contributing something useful.

A strong professional identity develops through consistent actions, not just an impressive profile.
