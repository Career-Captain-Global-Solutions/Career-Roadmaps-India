# Product Management Career Roadmap in India: Roles, Skills and Portfolio

**An educational resource by Career Captain Global Solutions**

Product management focuses on identifying user and business problems, setting product direction and coordinating teams that build and improve products. A product manager is not simply a project manager or the person who writes feature requests. The role involves discovery, prioritisation, trade-offs and outcome measurement.

## 1. What product managers do

Depending on the organisation, responsibilities can include:
- Researching users, customers and market problems.
- Defining product goals and success measures.
- Maintaining and prioritising a product backlog or roadmap.
- Clarifying requirements with design and engineering teams.
- Evaluating trade-offs involving time, cost, risk and user value.
- Reviewing product usage, feedback and performance.
- Coordinating launches and communicating decisions.

Product management differs across consumer apps, enterprise software, fintech, health technology and industrial products.

## 2. Skills to develop

- User research and problem discovery.
- Product thinking and prioritisation.
- Clear written communication.
- Data interpretation and experimentation literacy.
- Business models, markets and customer understanding.
- Collaboration with design, engineering, sales and support.
- Technical fluency appropriate to the product domain.

A product manager may not code, but should understand technical constraints and communicate effectively with technical teams.

## 3. Understand the product lifecycle

1. Discover a user or business problem.
2. Validate the problem with evidence.
3. Define the intended outcome and target users.
4. Explore and prioritise possible solutions.
5. Coordinate design, engineering and testing.
6. Launch, measure and learn.
7. Improve or discontinue features based on evidence.

## 4. Entry pathways

There is no single degree that automatically qualifies someone for product management. Relevant backgrounds can include engineering, business, design, economics and domain-specific study. Common adjacent entry roles include product analyst, business analyst, product operations associate, implementation specialist, customer success or domain specialist.

Build domain knowledge and evidence before targeting product manager titles. Some employers expect prior experience.

## 5. Portfolio projects

Create a product case study for a real problem:
- Define the user and problem.
- Show evidence from interviews, observation or credible research.
- Compare alternative solutions.
- Prioritise a small first release.
- Write sample user stories and acceptance criteria.
- Define measurable success indicators.
- Discuss risks, accessibility, privacy and trade-offs.

A polished mock-up alone is not proof of product judgement.

## 6. Metrics and responsible product decisions

Depending on the product, teams may review activation, task completion, retention, conversion, reliability, customer satisfaction and support burden. Metrics need context; a single number can hide user harm or poor quality.

Consider privacy, accessibility, security, fairness and regulatory obligations from the start.

## 7. Interview preparation

Prepare examples of:
- A problem you identified and investigated.
- A decision made with incomplete information.
- A prioritisation trade-off.
- A disagreement resolved through evidence.
- A product or service you would improve, with a reasoned proposal.

## 8. 90-day preparation plan

Month 1: Study product fundamentals, user research and role descriptions.

Month 2: Complete one detailed product case study and practise data interpretation.

Month 3: Get feedback, improve the case study, build domain knowledge and apply to realistic adjacent or entry-level roles.

Use employer career pages and the National Career Service (https://www.ncs.gov.in/) to review current requirements.
