Business analysts help organisations understand problems, clarify requirements and improve processes or technology solutions. The role sits between business stakeholders, users and delivery teams. It is not always a non-technical role: some positions require SQL, data analysis or familiarity with enterprise systems.

## 1. What a business analyst does

Typical activities may include:
- Interviewing stakeholders and understanding business needs.
- Mapping current and proposed processes.
- Documenting requirements, assumptions and constraints.
- Writing user stories and acceptance criteria.
- Supporting prioritisation and change management.
- Coordinating with design, development, testing and operations teams.
- Reviewing whether a delivered solution addresses the original need.

Responsibilities differ by industry and seniority. A business analyst in banking may work with regulatory processes; one in software may focus on product requirements.

## 2. Core skills

**Analytical:** problem framing, structured thinking, root-cause analysis and data interpretation.

**Communication:** interviews, workshops, presentations, clear writing and active listening.

**Process:** process maps, workflows, requirements traceability and change documentation.

**Technical awareness:** spreadsheets, databases, SQL basics, software delivery concepts and reporting tools where relevant.

**Professional:** stakeholder management, facilitation, prioritisation and attention to detail.

## 3. Common tools and methods

Tools vary by employer. You may encounter spreadsheets, presentation software, diagramming tools, Jira or similar work trackers, SQL clients and business intelligence platforms. Learn the underlying concepts before collecting tool badges.

Useful methods include:
- Stakeholder analysis.
- As-is and to-be process mapping.
- Functional and non-functional requirements.
- User stories and acceptance criteria.
- Use cases and simple data-flow diagrams.
- UAT planning and defect triage.

## 4. How to enter the field

Graduates from engineering, business, commerce, science and other disciplines may find relevant entry routes, depending on employer criteria. Potential starting roles include business analyst trainee, operations analyst, implementation analyst, reporting analyst, process associate or domain support.

Build evidence through:
1. A process-improvement case study.
2. A sample requirements document for a clearly labelled hypothetical product.
3. A process map and user stories.
4. A small data analysis using a public dataset.
5. An explanation of how you validated assumptions.

Do not claim a simulated project was client work.

## 5. Example portfolio case study

Choose a familiar process, such as a college event registration workflow. Describe the problem, stakeholders, current steps, pain points, proposed workflow, requirements, acceptance criteria and success measures. State assumptions and limitations.

## 6. Interview preparation

Practise answering:
- How would you clarify an unclear requirement?
- How would you handle two stakeholders with conflicting priorities?
- What is the difference between a requirement and a solution?
- How would you map a process?
- How do you know whether a proposed change worked?

Use specific examples and explain your reasoning.

## 7. 60-day learning plan

Weeks 1–2: Learn business analysis fundamentals and analyse job descriptions.

Weeks 3–4: Practise process mapping, requirements and user stories.

Weeks 5–6: Learn spreadsheet analysis and introductory SQL if relevant to target roles.

Weeks 7–8: Complete a portfolio case study, prepare your CV and practise interviews.

## 8. Opportunity research

Use the National Career Service portal (https://www.ncs.gov.in/) and employer career pages to understand role titles and requirements. Compare multiple job descriptions; titles are not standardised.
