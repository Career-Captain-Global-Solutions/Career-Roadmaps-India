# UI/UX Designer Career Roadmap in India: Skills, Process and Portfolio

**An educational resource by Career Captain Global Solutions**

UI design concerns the visual and interactive interface of a digital product. UX design focuses on the quality of the user's overall experience, including needs, tasks, information, usability and accessibility. In many organisations the responsibilities overlap; in others they are separate specialisations.

## 1. What designers work on

A designer may:
- Research users and understand tasks.
- Analyse journeys and pain points.
- Organise information and navigation.
- Sketch flows and wireframes.
- Create interface layouts and interactive prototypes.
- Test usability and refine designs.
- Work with product managers and developers.
- Review implementation and design consistency.

## 2. Essential capabilities

- User-centred problem solving.
- Research planning and interview skills.
- Information architecture and interaction design.
- Visual hierarchy, typography, layout and colour.
- Wireframing and prototyping.
- Usability testing and feedback analysis.
- Accessibility awareness and inclusive design.
- Design documentation and collaboration.

Tools change over time. Learn design principles and workflows rather than relying on a particular application alone.

## 3. A practical design process

1. Define the user, context and problem.
2. Gather evidence through research and observation.
3. Map tasks, journeys and information.
4. Explore multiple solution concepts.
5. Build wireframes and prototypes.
6. Test with representative users.
7. Iterate and document decisions.
8. Collaborate on implementation and review.

Do not treat personal preference as a substitute for user evidence.

## 4. Build a strong portfolio

Create two or three detailed case studies. Each should explain:
- The problem and intended users.
- Your role and project constraints.
- Research method and findings.
- Key design decisions and alternatives.
- Wireframes, prototypes and final screens.
- Usability feedback and iterations.
- Accessibility considerations.
- What you would improve next.

Clearly identify student, personal, volunteer and professional work. Do not invent client results or user research.

## 5. Learning and entry routes

Students from design, engineering, computer science, psychology, communication and other backgrounds may explore UX/UI, depending on their skills and portfolio. Entry titles can include UI designer, UX designer, visual designer, interaction designer, UX researcher assistant or product design intern.

Review employer requirements for visual design, research, prototyping, accessibility and collaboration.

## 6. Interview preparation

Be ready to walk through a case study, explain trade-offs, respond to critique and describe how research changed your design. Practise a short portfolio presentation that distinguishes your contribution from team work.

## 7. Eight-week portfolio plan

Weeks 1–2: Learn design fundamentals and accessibility basics.

Weeks 3–4: Research a small, well-defined user problem.

Weeks 5–6: Build flows, wireframes and a clickable prototype.

Weeks 7–8: Test, iterate, document and publish the case study.
