# Interview Preparation Guide for Freshers in India: Technical and HR Rounds

**An educational resource by Career Captain Global Solutions**

An interview is a structured conversation in which an employer evaluates how your knowledge, experience and approach relate to a role. Preparation is not about memorising perfect scripts. It is about understanding the role, presenting accurate examples and communicating your reasoning.

## 1. Research before the interview

- Read the job description and identify essential responsibilities.
- Understand the organisation's products, services and sector.
- Review the interview format and instructions supplied by the recruiter.
- Prepare questions about the role, team, expectations and learning support.
- Verify the interview invitation through official company channels if anything appears suspicious.

## 2. Prepare your introduction

A concise introduction can include:
1. Your current educational background.
2. One or two relevant skills or areas of interest.
3. A project, internship or experience that demonstrates them.
4. Why the role is relevant to your next step.

Avoid reciting personal details unrelated to the job or making claims you cannot support.

## 3. Use structured examples

For behavioural questions, the STAR approach can help:
- **Situation:** Briefly describe the context.
- **Task:** Explain your responsibility.
- **Action:** Describe what you personally did.
- **Result:** State the outcome and what you learned.

Use real examples from projects, college activities, volunteering or work. Be clear about team contributions versus your own.

## 4. Technical or role-specific rounds

Review fundamentals that appear in the job description. Practise explaining:
- How a project works.
- Why you selected a method or tool.
- A challenge, error or disagreement.
- How you tested or checked the result.
- What you would improve.

For non-technical roles, prepare relevant case discussions, writing samples, presentations or domain questions.

## 5. Common questions to practise

- Tell me about yourself.
- Why are you interested in this role?
- Explain a project you worked on.
- What did you learn from a mistake?
- How do you manage competing deadlines?
- Describe a time you worked with others.
- What would you like to learn in this role?

## 6. Questions you can ask the interviewer

Ask about day-to-day responsibilities, onboarding, success measures, team structure, tools and the next steps in the process. Avoid asking for information already clearly provided.

## 7. Online interview checklist

- Test microphone, camera, internet and meeting link.
- Choose a quiet, well-lit location.
- Keep your resume and relevant notes accessible.
- Join according to the instructions.
- Do not record or share an interview without permission.

## 8. After the interview

Send a brief thank-you message if appropriate, note questions you found difficult and update your application tracker. Do not repeatedly contact interviewers or assume silence means a particular outcome.
