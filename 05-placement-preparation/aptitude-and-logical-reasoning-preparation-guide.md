# Aptitude and Logical Reasoning Preparation for Campus Placements

**An educational resource by Career Captain Global Solutions**

Many campus recruitment processes include aptitude, reasoning or role-specific assessments, but formats differ by employer. Preparation should be based on the actual test pattern and role rather than a generic promise that every company asks the same questions.

## 1. Common assessment areas

### Quantitative aptitude
- Percentages, ratios and proportions.
- Averages, profit and loss.
- Time, speed and distance.
- Time and work.
- Basic algebra and number systems.
- Data interpretation using tables and charts.

### Logical reasoning
- Sequences and patterns.
- Arrangements and ordering.
- Syllogisms and deductions.
- Coding-decoding and classification.
- Directions and basic analytical puzzles.

### Verbal ability
- Reading comprehension.
- Grammar and sentence correction.
- Vocabulary in context.
- Paragraph ordering and written clarity.

### Role-specific tests
Engineering, finance, analytics, design and technology roles may include subject or practical assessments. Coding tests apply to some roles, not all.

## 2. A diagnostic-first method

1. Take a timed diagnostic using a reputable practice set.
2. Record accuracy, time and the question types you found difficult.
3. Separate concept gaps from careless errors and time-management issues.
4. Prioritise two weak areas at a time.
5. Retest weekly under similar conditions.

## 3. A four-week study plan

**Week 1:** Diagnose; revise arithmetic foundations and reading comprehension.

**Week 2:** Practise ratios, percentages, averages, basic algebra and core reasoning patterns.

**Week 3:** Add timed mixed sets and data interpretation. Review every incorrect answer.

**Week 4:** Take mock tests in the format and time limit specified by your target employer, where that information is available.

Adjust the plan to your starting level and exam date.

## 4. How to review mistakes

For every incorrect or slow question, note:
- Topic and question type.
- Why your first approach failed.
- Correct concept or method.
- Whether the issue was knowledge, interpretation or time.
- One similar question to confirm learning.

Avoid memorising answer keys without understanding the method.

## 5. Test-day strategy

Read instructions carefully. Check whether negative marking applies. Allocate time, skip questions that are consuming too much time and return if the platform permits. Do not assume all tests allow backtracking or use the same scoring rules.
